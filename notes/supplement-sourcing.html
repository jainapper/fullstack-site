03 / Restrictions

Three things you can't simply add to a cart here

Australia regulates supplements more tightly than the US, and a stack guide written overseas will happily recommend things you can't legally order. If any of these are in your document, this is why the link 404s at checkout.

Melatonin

Prescription-only in Australia, with two narrow exceptions available from a pharmacist without a script: modified-release tablets of 2 mg or less for adults 55 and over, and immediate-release preparations of 5 mg or less for jet lag in adults 18 and over. Importing it is where people get caught — the TGA has issued repeated safety alerts about counterfeit melatonin sold to Australians online, and in early 2026 escalated enforcement, seizing over 12 tonnes of non-compliant product and fining a major marketplace $150,000 over Schedule 4 breaches. Even with a valid prescription, counterfeit product can't be brought in under the Personal Importation Scheme. Get it from a pharmacy.

NMN

Status changed recently, in your favour. NMN formulations are now lawfully listed on the ARTG — Bioglan NMN 500 was registered in December 2025 — so you can buy it locally rather than importing. Prices remain high and the evidence remains thin; treat it as optional.

SARMs & peptides

If the document recommends anything in this family, it isn't a supplement question. These are prescription substances or unapproved goods in Australia and importing them carries genuine legal risk on top of the health one. There is no retailer to point you at.

04 / Quality

Four checks before you pay

Thirty seconds each, and they eliminate most of what goes wrong with buying supplements online.

AUST L / AUST R

Anything legally sold in Australia carries a TGA listing number on the label — AUST L for listed, AUST R for registered. Its absence on a domestic product is a warning sign. Imports bought through the Personal Importation Scheme won't have one, which is expected, but means nobody has vetted it for you.

Batch testing

HASTA is Australia's certification body and the only local lab independently certifying sports supplements against the WADA prohibited list. Informed Sport and NSF Certified for Sport are the international equivalents; NSF screens each batch against 290+ substances. Relevant if you're tested for sport, and a decent proxy for manufacturing care even if you aren't.

Verify the claim

A certification logo on a label means nothing on its own. Each program publishes a searchable list of certified products — if it isn't on the certifier's own website, treat the logo as decoration. HASTA-certified products carry batch-specific numbers you can look up.

Read the back panel

Front-of-pack numbers describe the compound; the back describes the dose. "1000 mg fish oil" can mean 300 mg of EPA+DHA, and "500 mg magnesium citrate" can mean 80 mg of elemental magnesium. Compare products on the actives, and work out cost per serve before deciding anything is cheap.
